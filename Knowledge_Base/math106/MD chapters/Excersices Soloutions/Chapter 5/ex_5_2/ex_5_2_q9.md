## Problem
Sketch the region $R$ bounded by the graphs of the equations, and find the volume of the solid generated if $R$ is revolved about the indicated axis.
$y = x^2$, $y = 2$; y-axis

## Rule Used
Definition 5.6 / Volume $V$ of a Circular Disk 5.7

## Solution
**Step 1 — Express $x$ in terms of $y$**
Since the region is revolved around the y-axis, we use horizontal rectangles, resulting in disks with thickness $dy$.
$$y = x^2 \implies x = \pm \sqrt{y}$$
The region bounded by the y-axis revolves to form the same solid regardless of which branch is chosen. The radius is $g(y) = \sqrt{y}$.

**Step 2 — Set up the integral for volume**
The limits of integration are from $y = 0$ (the vertex) to $y = 2$.
$$V = \int_{0}^{2} \pi [g(y)]^2 dy$$
$$V = \int_{0}^{2} \pi (\sqrt{y})^2 dy$$
$$V = \pi \int_{0}^{2} y dy$$

**Step 3 — Evaluate the definite integral**
$$V = \pi \left[ \frac{y^2}{2} \right]_0^2$$
$$V = \pi \left( \frac{2^2}{2} - \frac{0^2}{2} \right)$$
$$V = 2\pi$$

## Answer
$2\pi$

## Check by Differentiation
$\frac{d}{dy} \left[ \pi \frac{y^2}{2} \right] = \pi y = \pi (\sqrt{y})^2$. Verified.
