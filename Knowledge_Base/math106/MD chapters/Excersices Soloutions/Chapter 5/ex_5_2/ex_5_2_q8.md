## Problem
Sketch the region $R$ bounded by the graphs of the equations, and find the volume of the solid generated if $R$ is revolved about the indicated axis.
$y = x^3$, $x = -2$, $y = 0$; x-axis

## Rule Used
Definition 5.5 / Volume $V$ of a Circular Disk 5.7

## Solution
**Step 1 — Identify the components of the disk**
The region is below the x-axis from $x = -2$ to $x = 0$.
When revolved around the x-axis, the radius is the absolute value of the function: $\text{radius} = |x^3|$.
Since we square the radius, the sign does not matter: $(\text{radius})^2 = (x^3)^2 = x^6$.
The bounds are $x = -2$ to $x = 0$.

**Step 2 — Set up the integral for volume**
$$V = \int_{-2}^{0} \pi [f(x)]^2 dx$$
$$V = \int_{-2}^{0} \pi (x^3)^2 dx$$
$$V = \pi \int_{-2}^{0} x^6 dx$$

**Step 3 — Evaluate the definite integral**
$$V = \pi \left[ \frac{x^7}{7} \right]_{-2}^0$$
$$V = \pi \left( \frac{0^7}{7} - \frac{(-2)^7}{7} \right)$$
$$V = \pi \left( 0 - \frac{-128}{7} \right) = \frac{128\pi}{7}$$

## Answer
$\frac{128\pi}{7}$

## Check by Differentiation
$\frac{d}{dx} \left[ \pi \frac{x^7}{7} \right] = \pi x^6 = \pi (x^3)^2$. Verified.
