## Problem
Sketch the region $R$ bounded by the graphs of the equations, and find the volume of the solid generated if $R$ is revolved about the indicated axis. Use cylindrical shells.
$y = 2x$, $y = 6$, $x = 0$; x-axis

## Rule Used
Guidelines for Finding the Volume of a Solid of Revolution Using Cylindrical Shells 5.12

## Solution
**Step 1 — Choose the appropriate slicing direction**
To use cylindrical shells around the x-axis, we use horizontal rectangles of thickness $dy$. 

**Step 2 — Identify the components of the shell**
The radius of the shell is the distance to the x-axis: $\text{radius} = y$.
The altitude of the shell is determined by the right and left boundaries. We rewrite $y = 2x$ as $x = \frac{y}{2}$.
The right boundary is the line $x = \frac{y}{2}$ and the left boundary is the y-axis $x = 0$.
$$\text{altitude} = \frac{y}{2} - 0 = \frac{y}{2}$$
The limits of integration for $y$ go from the origin ($y = 0$) up to the line $y = 6$.

**Step 3 — Set up the integral for volume**
$$V = \int_{0}^{6} 2\pi y \left(\frac{y}{2}\right) dy$$
$$V = \pi \int_{0}^{6} y^2 dy$$

**Step 4 — Evaluate the definite integral**
$$V = \pi \left[ \frac{y^3}{3} \right]_0^6$$
$$V = \pi \left( \frac{6^3}{3} \right) = \pi \left( \frac{216}{3} \right) = 72\pi$$

## Answer
$72\pi$

## Check by Differentiation
$\frac{d}{dy} \left[ \pi \frac{y^3}{3} \right] = \pi y^2 = 2\pi y \left( \frac{y}{2} \right)$. Verified.
