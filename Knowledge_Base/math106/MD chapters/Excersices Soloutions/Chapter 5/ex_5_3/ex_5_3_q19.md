## Problem
Let $R$ be the region bounded by the graphs of the equations. Set up an integral that can be used to find the volume of the solid generated if $R$ is revolved about the given line. Use cylindrical shells.
$y = x^2 + 1$, $x = 0$, $x = 2$, $y = 0$
(a) $x = 3$
(b) $x = -1$

## Rule Used
Guidelines for Finding the Volume of a Solid of Revolution Using Cylindrical Shells 5.12

## Solution
**Step 1 — General characteristics of the Shells**
Because we are revolving around vertical lines ($x = k$) and using the cylindrical shells method, we will use vertical rectangles of thickness $dx$.
The altitude of the shell remains the same for both parts: the distance from the upper curve to the lower curve.
$$\text{altitude} = (x^2 + 1) - 0 = x^2 + 1$$
The bounds of integration for $x$ are given by the domain of the region: $x = 0$ to $x = 2$.

**Part (a) — Revolving about $x = 3$**
**Step 2 — Determine the radius**
The axis of revolution ($x = 3$) lies to the right of the region. The average radius of the shell is the distance from a generic $x$ in $[0, 2]$ to the line $x = 3$:
$$\text{radius} = 3 - x$$

**Step 3 — Construct the Integral**
$$V = \int_{0}^{2} 2\pi (3 - x)(x^2 + 1) dx$$

**Part (b) — Revolving about $x = -1$**
**Step 4 — Determine the radius**
The axis of revolution ($x = -1$) lies to the left of the region. The average radius of the shell is the distance from the line $x = -1$ to a generic $x$ in $[0, 2]$:
$$\text{radius} = x - (-1) = x + 1$$

**Step 5 — Construct the Integral**
$$V = \int_{0}^{2} 2\pi (x + 1)(x^2 + 1) dx$$

## Answer
(a) $\int_{0}^{2} 2\pi (3 - x)(x^2 + 1) dx$
(b) $\int_{0}^{2} 2\pi (x + 1)(x^2 + 1) dx$
