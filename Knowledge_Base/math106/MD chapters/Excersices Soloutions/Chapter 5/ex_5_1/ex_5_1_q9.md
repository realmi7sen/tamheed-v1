## Problem
Sketch the region bounded by the graphs of the equations and find its area: 
$y = 1/x^2$; $y = -x^2$; $x = 1$; $x = 2$

## Rule Used
Guidelines for Finding the Area of an $R_x$ Region (5.3)

## Solution
**Step 1 — Identify boundaries on the given interval**
The interval is given strictly by the vertical lines $x = 1$ to $x = 2$.
On $[1, 2]$, the function $y = 1/x^2$ is strictly positive, and $y = -x^2$ is strictly negative.
Upper boundary: $f(x) = \frac{1}{x^2} = x^{-2}$
Lower boundary: $g(x) = -x^2$

**Step 2 — Set up the definite integral**
$$A = \int_{1}^{2} \left[ x^{-2} - (-x^2) \right] dx = \int_{1}^{2} (x^{-2} + x^2) dx$$

**Step 3 — Evaluate the integral**
$$A = \left[ -x^{-1} + \frac{x^3}{3} \right]_1^2 = \left[ -\frac{1}{x} + \frac{x^3}{3} \right]_1^2$$
$$A = \left( -\frac{1}{2} + \frac{8}{3} \right) - \left( -1 + \frac{1}{3} \right)$$
$$A = \left( \frac{-3 + 16}{6} \right) - \left( -\frac{2}{3} \right) = \frac{13}{6} + \frac{4}{6} = \frac{17}{6}$$

## Answer
$\frac{17}{6}$

## Check by Differentiation
Verify the antiderivative:
$\frac{d}{dx} \left[ -x^{-1} + \frac{x^3}{3} \right] = x^{-2} + x^2$. Verified.
