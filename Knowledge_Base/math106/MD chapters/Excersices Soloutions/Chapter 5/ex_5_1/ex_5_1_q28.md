## Problem
Set up sums of integrals that can be used to find the area of the region bounded by the graphs of the equations by integrating with respect to (a) x and (b) y.
$y = 1 - x^2$; $y = x - 1$

## Rule Used
Guidelines for Finding the Area of an $R_x$ Region (5.3) and an $R_y$ Region (5.4)

## Solution
**Step 1 — Find points of intersection**
$$1 - x^2 = x - 1 \Rightarrow x^2 + x - 2 = 0 \Rightarrow (x + 2)(x - 1) = 0$$
Intersection x-coordinates: $x = -2$ and $x = 1$.
Corresponding y-coordinates: at $x = -2, y = -3$. At $x = 1, y = 0$.

**Part (a) — Integrating with respect to x**
**Step 2 — Identify Boundaries:**
On interval $[-2, 1]$, $1 - x^2$ is an inverted parabola, so it forms the upper boundary.
Upper bound: $1 - x^2$. Lower bound: $x - 1$.
**Step 3 — Construct Integral:**
$$A = \int_{-2}^{1} [(1 - x^2) - (x - 1)] dx$$

**Part (b) — Integrating with respect to y**
**Step 4 — Solve for x in terms of y:**
Parabola: $y = 1 - x^2 \Rightarrow x^2 = 1 - y \Rightarrow x = \pm\sqrt{1 - y}$ (valid for $y \le 1$).
Line: $y = x - 1 \Rightarrow x = y + 1$.
**Step 5 — Identify Left/Right Boundaries on y intervals:**
The $y$ values range from the lowest point at intersection $(-2, -3)$ to the peak of the parabola at $(0, 1)$. Thus, $y \in [-3, 1]$.
- For $y \in [-3, 0]$ (below the x-axis intersection): The right boundary is the line $x = y + 1$, and the left boundary is the left half of the parabola $x = -\sqrt{1 - y}$.
- For $y \in [0, 1]$ (above the x-axis): The right boundary is the right half of the parabola $x = \sqrt{1 - y}$, and the left boundary is the left half of the parabola $x = -\sqrt{1 - y}$.
**Step 6 — Construct Sum of Integrals:**
$$A = \int_{-3}^{0} [(y + 1) - (-\sqrt{1 - y})] dy + \int_{0}^{1} [\sqrt{1 - y} - (-\sqrt{1 - y})] dy$$

## Answer
(a) $\int_{-2}^{1} [(1 - x^2) - (x - 1)] dx$
(b) $\int_{-3}^{0} [y + 1 + \sqrt{1 - y}] dy + \int_{0}^{1} [2\sqrt{1 - y}] dy$
