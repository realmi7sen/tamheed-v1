## Problem
Sketch the region bounded by the graphs of the equations and find its area: 
$y^2 = -x$; $x - y = 4$; $y = -1$; $y = 2$

## Rule Used
Guidelines for Finding the Area of an $R_y$ Region (5.4)

## Solution
**Step 1 — Express $x$ in terms of $y$**
Left-most/right-most boundaries are easier assessed relative to the y-axis.
$x = -y^2$
$x = y + 4$

**Step 2 — Identify boundaries on the given interval**
The interval given is $y \in [-1, 2]$.
Test $y = 0$: $x = 0 + 4 = 4$, and $x = -0^2 = 0$.
Since $4 > 0$, the right boundary is $f(y) = y + 4$ and the left boundary is $g(y) = -y^2$.

**Step 3 — Set up the definite integral**
$$A = \int_{-1}^{2} [(y + 4) - (-y^2)] dy = \int_{-1}^{2} (y^2 + y + 4) dy$$

**Step 4 — Evaluate the integral**
$$A = \left[ \frac{y^3}{3} + \frac{y^2}{2} + 4y \right]_{-1}^2$$
$$A = \left( \frac{8}{3} + \frac{4}{2} + 8 \right) - \left( -\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{2} - 4 \right)$$
$$A = \left( \frac{8}{3} + 10 \right) - \left( -\frac{1}{3} - \frac{7}{2} \right)$$
$$A = \frac{38}{3} - \left( \frac{-2 - 21}{6} \right) = \frac{76}{6} + \frac{23}{6} = \frac{99}{6} = \frac{33}{2}$$

## Answer
$\frac{33}{2}$

## Check by Differentiation
Verify the antiderivative:
$\frac{d}{dy} \left[ \frac{y^3}{3} + \frac{y^2}{2} + 4y \right] = y^2 + y + 4$. Verified.
