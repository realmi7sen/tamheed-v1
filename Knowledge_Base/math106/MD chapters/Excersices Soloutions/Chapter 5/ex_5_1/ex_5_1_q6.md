## Problem
Sketch the region bounded by the graphs of the equations and find its area: 
$x + y = 3$; $y + x^2 = 3$

## Rule Used
Guidelines for Finding the Area of an $R_x$ Region (5.3)

## Solution
**Step 1 — Express $y$ in terms of $x$**
Rewrite the equations as:
$y = 3 - x$
$y = 3 - x^2$

**Step 2 — Find the points of intersection**
Set the equations equal to each other:
$$3 - x = 3 - x^2$$
$$x^2 - x = 0$$
$$x(x - 1) = 0$$
The intersections are at $x = 0$ and $x = 1$.

**Step 3 — Identify boundaries**
On the interval $[0, 1]$, $3 - x^2 \ge 3 - x$. 
Upper boundary: $f(x) = 3 - x^2$
Lower boundary: $g(x) = 3 - x$

**Step 4 — Set up the definite integral**
$$A = \int_{0}^{1} [(3 - x^2) - (3 - x)] dx = \int_{0}^{1} (x - x^2) dx$$

**Step 5 — Evaluate the integral**
$$A = \left[ \frac{x^2}{2} - \frac{x^3}{3} \right]_0^1$$
$$A = \left( \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{3} \right) - 0 = \frac{3}{6} - \frac{2}{6} = \frac{1}{6}$$

## Answer
$\frac{1}{6}$

## Check by Differentiation
Verify the antiderivative:
$\frac{d}{dx} \left[ \frac{x^2}{2} - \frac{x^3}{3} \right] = x - x^2$. Verified.
