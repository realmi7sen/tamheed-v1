## Problem
Find the area of the region between the graphs of $f$ and $g$ if $x$ is restricted to the given interval:
$f(x) = 6 - 3x^2$; $g(x) = 3x$; $[0, 2]$

## Rule Used
Theorem 5.1 (Area between intersecting graphs crossing within interval)

## Solution
**Step 1 — Find points of intersection within the interval**
Set the functions equal to find any crossover points:
$$6 - 3x^2 = 3x$$
$$3x^2 + 3x - 6 = 0$$
$$3(x^2 + x - 2) = 0$$
$$3(x + 2)(x - 1) = 0$$
The functions cross at $x = -2$ and $x = 1$. The only point within $[0, 2]$ is $x = 1$.

**Step 2 — Determine upper and lower bounds on each sub-interval**
The area must be split into two integrals: one for $[0, 1]$ and one for $[1, 2]$.
- On $[0, 1]$: Test $x = 0$. $f(0) = 6$, $g(0) = 0$. $f(x) \ge g(x)$.
- On $[1, 2]$: Test $x = 2$. $f(2) = 6 - 12 = -6$, $g(2) = 6$. $g(x) \ge f(x)$.

**Step 3 — Set up the integrals**
$$A = \int_{0}^{1} (f(x) - g(x)) dx + \int_{1}^{2} (g(x) - f(x)) dx$$
$$A = \int_{0}^{1} (6 - 3x^2 - 3x) dx + \int_{1}^{2} (3x - (6 - 3x^2)) dx$$

**Step 4 — Evaluate the first integral $[0, 1]$**
$$\int_{0}^{1} (6 - 3x^2 - 3x) dx = \left[ 6x - x^3 - \frac{3x^2}{2} \right]_0^1$$
$$= \left( 6 - 1 - \frac{3}{2} \right) - 0 = 5 - 1.5 = 3.5 = \frac{7}{2}$$

**Step 5 — Evaluate the second integral $[1, 2]$**
$$\int_{1}^{2} (3x^2 + 3x - 6) dx = \left[ x^3 + \frac{3x^2}{2} - 6x \right]_1^2$$
$$= \left( (2)^3 + \frac{3(4)}{2} - 6(2) \right) - \left( 1 + \frac{3}{2} - 6 \right)$$
$$= (8 + 6 - 12) - \left( -3.5 \right) = 2 + 3.5 = 5.5 = \frac{11}{2}$$

**Step 6 — Add the areas**
$$A = \frac{7}{2} + \frac{11}{2} = \frac{18}{2} = 9$$

## Answer
$9$

## Check by Differentiation
Verify antiderivatives:
$\frac{d}{dx} \left[ 6x - x^3 - \frac{3x^2}{2} \right] = 6 - 3x^2 - 3x$. Verified.
$\frac{d}{dx} \left[ x^3 + \frac{3x^2}{2} - 6x \right] = 3x^2 + 3x - 6$. Verified.
