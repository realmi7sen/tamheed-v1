## Problem
Curves $C_1, C_2, C_3$, and $C_4$ are given parametrically, for $t$ in $\mathbb{R}$. Sketch their graphs and indicate orientations.
$C_1: x = t^2, y = t$
$C_2: x = t^4, y = t^2$
$C_3: x = \sin^2 t, y = \sin t$
$C_4: x = e^{2t}, y = -e^t$

## Rule Used
Eliminating the parameter / Analyzing domain restrictions

## Solution
All four curves satisfy the basic Cartesian relationship $x = y^2$. However, their domains and orientations differ based on their specific parametrizations.

**Curve $C_1: x = t^2, y = t$**
- **Equation:** $x = y^2$
- **Domain:** Since $y = t$ and $t \in \mathbb{R}$, $y$ can be any real number. 
- **Graph & Orientation:** The graph is the complete parabola $x = y^2$ opening to the right. As $t$ increases, $y$ increases from $-\infty$ to $\infty$, meaning the curve is traced continuously from bottom to top.

**Curve $C_2: x = t^4, y = t^2$**
- **Equation:** Notice that $x = (t^2)^2 = y^2$. 
- **Domain:** Since $y = t^2$ and $t \in \mathbb{R}$, $y$ is restricted to $y \ge 0$.
- **Graph & Orientation:** The graph is only the top half of the parabola $x = y^2$ (in the first quadrant). As $t$ goes from $-\infty$ to $0$, $y$ goes from $\infty$ to $0$ (the point moves leftward and downward into the origin). As $t$ goes from $0$ to $\infty$, $y$ goes from $0$ to $\infty$ (the point retraces the same path outward).

**Curve $C_3: x = \sin^2 t, y = \sin t$**
- **Equation:** $x = (\sin t)^2 = y^2$.
- **Domain:** Since $y = \sin t$ and $t \in \mathbb{R}$, $y$ is restricted to the closed interval $[-1, 1]$.
- **Graph & Orientation:** The graph is a bounded segment of the parabola $x = y^2$ between the points $(1, -1)$ and $(1, 1)$. Because $\sin t$ is periodic, the point oscillates endlessly back and forth along this segment as $t$ increases.

**Curve $C_4: x = e^{2t}, y = -e^t$**
- **Equation:** $x = e^{2t} = (-(-e^t))^2 = y^2$.
- **Domain:** Since $e^t > 0$ for all real $t$, $y = -e^t$ strictly yields $y < 0$. 
- **Graph & Orientation:** The graph is only the bottom half of the parabola $x = y^2$ (in the fourth quadrant, excluding the origin). As $t$ goes from $-\infty$ to $\infty$, $y = -e^t$ goes from near $0$ down to $-\infty$. The point starts infinitely close to the origin and moves downward and to the right indefinitely.

## Answer
All curves lie on the parabola $x = y^2$.
* $C_1$: The full parabola, traced smoothly from bottom to top.
* $C_2$: The top half of the parabola ($y \ge 0$), traced inward from $\infty$ to the origin, then back outward to $\infty$.
* $C_3$: A bounded arc of the parabola between $(1, -1)$ and $(1, 1)$, traced back and forth endlessly.
* $C_4$: The bottom half of the parabola ($y < 0$, excluding origin), traced starting near the origin and moving outward toward $\infty$.
