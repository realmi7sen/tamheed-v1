## Problem
(a) Find an equation in $x$ and $y$ whose graph contains the points on the curve $C$. 
(b) Sketch the graph of $C$ and indicate the orientation.
$x = e^t$, $y = e^{-2t}$; $t$ in $\mathbb{R}$

## Rule Used
Eliminating the parameter

## Solution
**Step 1 — Express $y$ in terms of $x$ using exponent rules**
Notice the relationship between the bases:
$$y = e^{-2t} = (e^t)^{-2}$$

**Step 2 — Substitute $x$ into the equation**
Since $x = e^t$, replace $e^t$ with $x$:
$$y = x^{-2} = \frac{1}{x^2}$$

**Step 3 — Determine the domain and orientation**
Since $t \in \mathbb{R}$ and $x = e^t$, the range of possible $x$ values is strictly positive ($x > 0$). Thus, the curve is only the right half of the graph $y = \frac{1}{x^2}$.
As $t$ goes from $-\infty$ to $\infty$:
- $x = e^t$ goes from near $0$ to $\infty$.
- $y = e^{-2t}$ goes from $\infty$ down to near $0$.
Therefore, the orientation traces the curve starting from high up on the y-axis (near $x=0$) and moving out to the right towards the x-axis ($y=0$).

## Answer
(a) $y = \frac{1}{x^2}$ for $x > 0$
(b) The graph is the right branch of the reciprocal square curve in the first quadrant. The orientation moves rightward and downward, starting near the positive y-axis and asymptotically approaching the positive x-axis.

## Check by Differentiation
Substitute $x(t)$ into the derived Cartesian equation:
$y = \frac{1}{(e^t)^2} = \frac{1}{e^{2t}} = e^{-2t}$. This perfectly matches $y(t)$. Verified.
