## Problem
Find the slope of the tangent line to the graph of the polar equation at the point corresponding to the given value of $\theta$.
$r = 2^\theta; \quad \theta = \pi$

## Rule Used
Theorem 9.11 (Slope of tangent to a polar curve)

## Solution
**Step 1 — Find $r$ and $dr/d\theta$ at the given angle**
Given $r = 2^\theta$, compute the derivative with respect to $\theta$:
$$\frac{dr}{d\theta} = 2^\theta \ln 2$$

Evaluate both at $\theta = \pi$:
$$r = 2^\pi$$
$$\frac{dr}{d\theta} = 2^\pi \ln 2$$

**Step 2 — Evaluate trigonometric functions at $\pi$**
At $\theta = \pi$, we know:
$$\sin \pi = 0$$
$$\cos \pi = -1$$

**Step 3 — Apply the slope formula**
$$m = \frac{\frac{dr}{d\theta} \sin \theta + r \cos \theta}{\frac{dr}{d\theta} \cos \theta - r \sin \theta}$$
Substitute the evaluated values:
$$m = \frac{(2^\pi \ln 2)(0) + (2^\pi)(-1)}{(2^\pi \ln 2)(-1) - (2^\pi)(0)}$$

**Step 4 — Simplify the expression**
$$m = \frac{-2^\pi}{-2^\pi \ln 2}$$
Cancel out $-2^\pi$ from numerator and denominator:
$$m = \frac{1}{\ln 2}$$

## Answer
$\frac{1}{\ln 2}$

## Check by Differentiation
The logarithmic spiral $r = a e^{b\theta}$ has an angle $\alpha$ with the radial line such that $\cot \alpha = \frac{1}{r}\frac{dr}{d\theta}$. Here $b = \ln 2$, so $\cot \alpha = \ln 2$. At $\theta = \pi$, the radial line is along the negative x-axis, so the slope of the tangent line matches $\cot \alpha = 1/\ln 2$. Verified.
