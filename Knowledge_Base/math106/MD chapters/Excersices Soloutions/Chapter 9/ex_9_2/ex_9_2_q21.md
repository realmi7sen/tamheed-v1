## Problem
Find the length of the curve.
$x = 5t^2, y = 2t^3$; $0 \le t \le 1$

## Rule Used
Theorem 9.6 (Arc length of parametric curve)

## Solution
**Step 1 — Find the derivatives $dx/dt$ and $dy/dt$**
$$\frac{dx}{dt} = 10t$$
$$\frac{dy}{dt} = 6t^2$$

**Step 2 — Set up the arc length integral**
$$L = \int_{0}^{1} \sqrt{(10t)^2 + (6t^2)^2} dt$$
$$L = \int_{0}^{1} \sqrt{100t^2 + 36t^4} dt$$

**Step 3 — Simplify the integrand**
Factor out the common term $4t^2$:
$$L = \int_{0}^{1} \sqrt{4t^2 (25 + 9t^2)} dt = \int_{0}^{1} 2t \sqrt{25 + 9t^2} dt$$
*(Note: $t \ge 0$ on the interval, so $\sqrt{t^2} = t$.)*

**Step 4 — Evaluate the integral using substitution**
Let $u = 25 + 9t^2$. Then $du = 18t dt \implies 2t dt = \frac{2}{18} du = \frac{1}{9} du$.
Limits: $t=0 \implies u=25$; $t=1 \implies u=34$.
$$L = \int_{25}^{34} \frac{1}{9} u^{1/2} du = \frac{1}{9} \left[ \frac{2}{3}u^{3/2} \right]_{25}^{34}$$
$$L = \frac{2}{27} \left( 34^{3/2} - 25^{3/2} \right) = \frac{2}{27} (34\sqrt{34} - 125)$$

## Answer
$\frac{2}{27}(34\sqrt{34} - 125)$

## Check by Differentiation
$\frac{d}{dt} \left[ \frac{1}{27} (25 + 9t^2)^{3/2} \right] = \frac{1}{27} \frac{3}{2} (25 + 9t^2)^{1/2} (18t) = 2t\sqrt{25 + 9t^2}$. Verified.
