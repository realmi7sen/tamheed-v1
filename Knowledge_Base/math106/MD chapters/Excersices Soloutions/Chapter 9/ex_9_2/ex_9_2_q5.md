## Problem
Find the slopes of the tangent line and the normal line at the point on the curve that corresponds to $t = 1$.
$x = e^t, y = e^{-2t}$; $t$ in $\mathbb{R}$

## Rule Used
Theorem 9.4 (Derivative of Parametric Equations)

## Solution
**Step 1 — Find $dy/dt$ and $dx/dt$**
$$\frac{dx}{dt} = e^t$$
$$\frac{dy}{dt} = -2e^{-2t}$$

**Step 2 — Find the slope of the tangent line $dy/dx$ at $t = 1$**
Using Theorem 9.4:
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{-2e^{-2t}}{e^t} = -2e^{-3t}$$
Evaluate at $t = 1$:
$$m_{\text{tangent}} = -2e^{-3}$$

**Step 3 — Find the slope of the normal line**
The normal line's slope is the negative reciprocal:
$$m_{\text{normal}} = -\frac{1}{-2e^{-3}} = \frac{e^3}{2}$$

## Answer
Tangent slope: $\frac{-2}{e^3}$
Normal slope: $\frac{e^3}{2}$

## Check by Differentiation
Eliminate parameter: $y = x^{-2}$. $dy/dx = -2x^{-3}$. At $t=1$, $x=e$. Thus $dy/dx = -2e^{-3}$. Verified.
