## Problem
Find the slopes of the tangent line and the normal line at the point on the curve that corresponds to $t = 1$.
$x = t^2 + 1, y = t^2 - 1$; $-2 \le t \le 2$

## Rule Used
Theorem 9.4 (Derivative of Parametric Equations)

## Solution
**Step 1 — Find $dy/dt$ and $dx/dt$**
$$\frac{dx}{dt} = 2t$$
$$\frac{dy}{dt} = 2t$$

**Step 2 — Find the slope of the tangent line $dy/dx$ at $t = 1$**
Using Theorem 9.4:
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt} = \frac{2t}{2t} = 1 \quad (\text{for } t \ne 0)$$
Evaluate at $t = 1$:
$$m_{\text{tangent}} = 1$$

**Step 3 — Find the slope of the normal line**
The normal line is perpendicular to the tangent line, so its slope is the negative reciprocal:
$$m_{\text{normal}} = -\frac{1}{1} = -1$$

## Answer
Tangent slope: $1$
Normal slope: $-1$

## Check by Differentiation
Eliminate the parameter: $y = x - 2$. The slope of $y = x - 2$ is $1$ everywhere. Verified.
