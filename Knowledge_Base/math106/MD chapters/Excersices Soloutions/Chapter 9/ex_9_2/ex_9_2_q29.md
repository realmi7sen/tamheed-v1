## Problem
Find the area of the surface generated by revolving the curve about the x-axis.
$x = t^2, y = 2t$; $0 \le t \le 4$

## Rule Used
Theorem 9.8 (Surface Area for Revolution about the x-axis)

## Solution
**Step 1 — Find the derivatives $dx/dt$ and $dy/dt$**
$$\frac{dx}{dt} = 2t$$
$$\frac{dy}{dt} = 2$$

**Step 2 — Construct the arc length differential $ds$**
$$ds = \sqrt{(2t)^2 + (2)^2} dt = \sqrt{4t^2 + 4} dt = 2\sqrt{t^2 + 1} dt$$

**Step 3 — Set up the surface area integral**
$$S = \int_{0}^{4} 2\pi y ds = \int_{0}^{4} 2\pi (2t) \left( 2\sqrt{t^2 + 1} \right) dt$$
$$S = 8\pi \int_{0}^{4} t\sqrt{t^2 + 1} dt$$

**Step 4 — Evaluate the integral**
Let $u = t^2 + 1$, then $du = 2t dt \implies t dt = \frac{1}{2} du$.
Limits: $t=0 \implies u=1$; $t=4 \implies u=17$.
$$S = 8\pi \int_{1}^{17} \frac{1}{2} u^{1/2} du = 4\pi \left[ \frac{2}{3}u^{3/2} \right]_1^{17}$$
$$S = \frac{8\pi}{3} (17^{3/2} - 1^{3/2}) = \frac{8\pi}{3}(17\sqrt{17} - 1)$$

## Answer
$\frac{8\pi}{3}(17\sqrt{17} - 1)$

## Check by Differentiation
$\frac{d}{dt} \left[ \frac{8\pi}{3} (t^2 + 1)^{3/2} \right] = \frac{8\pi}{3} \cdot \frac{3}{2} (t^2+1)^{1/2} \cdot (2t) = 8\pi t \sqrt{t^2+1}$. Verified.
