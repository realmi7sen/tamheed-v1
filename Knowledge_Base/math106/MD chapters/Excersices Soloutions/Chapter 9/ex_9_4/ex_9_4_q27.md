## Problem
Find the length of the curve.
$r = e^{-\theta}$ from $\theta = 0$ to $\theta = 2\pi$

## Rule Used
Differential of Arc Length in Polar Coordinates 9.14

## Solution
**Step 1 — Find the derivative $dr/d\theta$**
$$r = e^{-\theta}$$
$$\frac{dr}{d\theta} = -e^{-\theta}$$

**Step 2 — Set up the arc length differential $ds$**
$$ds = \sqrt{r^2 + \left(\frac{dr}{d\theta}\right)^2} d\theta$$
$$ds = \sqrt{(e^{-\theta})^2 + (-e^{-\theta})^2} d\theta$$
$$ds = \sqrt{e^{-2\theta} + e^{-2\theta}} d\theta = \sqrt{2e^{-2\theta}} d\theta = \sqrt{2} e^{-\theta} d\theta$$

**Step 3 — Integrate to find length**
$$L = \int_{0}^{2\pi} \sqrt{2} e^{-\theta} d\theta$$
$$L = \sqrt{2} \left[ -e^{-\theta} \right]_0^{2\pi}$$

**Step 4 — Evaluate**
$$L = \sqrt{2} ( -e^{-2\pi} - (-e^0) )$$
$$L = \sqrt{2} (1 - e^{-2\pi})$$

## Answer
$\sqrt{2}(1 - e^{-2\pi})$

## Check by Differentiation
$\frac{d}{d\theta} [-\sqrt{2} e^{-\theta}] = \sqrt{2} e^{-\theta}$. Verified.
