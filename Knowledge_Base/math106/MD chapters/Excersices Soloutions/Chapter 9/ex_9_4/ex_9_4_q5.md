## Problem
Find the area of the region bounded by the graph of the polar equation.
$r = \sin 2\theta$

## Rule Used
Theorem 9.12 (Area of a Polar Region)

## Solution
**Step 1 — Determine the geometry and limits**
The graph of $r = \sin 2\theta$ is a four-leafed rose. Because the four leaves are symmetrical and identical in area, we can find the area of one leaf and multiply by $4$.
One leaf is traced out as $2\theta$ goes from $0$ to $\pi$, which corresponds to $\theta$ going from $0$ to $\pi/2$.

**Step 2 — Set up the integral**
The total area $A$ is $4$ times the area of one leaf:
$$A = 4 \left( \int_{0}^{\pi/2} \frac{1}{2} (\sin 2\theta)^2 d\theta \right)$$
$$A = 2 \int_{0}^{\pi/2} \sin^2 2\theta d\theta$$

**Step 3 — Apply identities and evaluate**
Use the identity $\sin^2 \alpha = \frac{1 - \cos 2\alpha}{2}$ with $\alpha = 2\theta$:
$$A = 2 \int_{0}^{\pi/2} \left( \frac{1 - \cos 4\theta}{2} \right) d\theta$$
$$A = \int_{0}^{\pi/2} (1 - \cos 4\theta) d\theta$$
$$A = \left[ \theta - \frac{1}{4} \sin 4\theta \right]_0^{\pi/2}$$
$$A = \left( \frac{\pi}{2} - \frac{1}{4} \sin 2\pi \right) - (0 - 0)$$
$$A = \frac{\pi}{2}$$

## Answer
$\frac{\pi}{2}$
