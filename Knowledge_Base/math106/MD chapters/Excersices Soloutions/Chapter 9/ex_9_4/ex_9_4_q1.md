## Problem
Find the area of the region bounded by the graph of the polar equation.
$r = 2 \cos \theta$

## Rule Used
Theorem 9.12 (Area of a Polar Region)

## Solution
**Step 1 — Determine the limits of integration**
The graph of $r = 2 \cos \theta$ is a circle passing through the pole. The entire circle is traced once as $\theta$ varies from $0$ to $\pi$. Thus, $\alpha = 0$ and $\beta = \pi$.

**Step 2 — Set up the integral**
Using Theorem 9.12:
$$A = \int_{0}^{\pi} \frac{1}{2} (2 \cos \theta)^2 d\theta$$
$$A = \int_{0}^{\pi} \frac{1}{2} (4 \cos^2 \theta) d\theta$$
$$A = \int_{0}^{\pi} 2 \cos^2 \theta d\theta$$

**Step 3 — Apply trigonometric identities and evaluate**
Use the half-angle identity $\cos^2 \theta = \frac{1 + \cos 2\theta}{2}$:
$$A = \int_{0}^{\pi} 2 \left( \frac{1 + \cos 2\theta}{2} \right) d\theta$$
$$A = \int_{0}^{\pi} (1 + \cos 2\theta) d\theta$$
$$A = \left[ \theta + \frac{1}{2} \sin 2\theta \right]_0^\pi$$
$$A = \left( \pi + \frac{1}{2} \sin 2\pi \right) - \left( 0 + \frac{1}{2} \sin 0 \right)$$
$$A = \pi + 0 - 0 = \pi$$

## Answer
$\pi$

## Check by Differentiation
Convert to rectangular coordinates: $r^2 = 2r \cos \theta \implies x^2 + y^2 = 2x \implies (x-1)^2 + y^2 = 1$. This is a circle of radius $1$. The area of a circle with $r=1$ is $\pi r^2 = \pi(1)^2 = \pi$. Verified.
