## Problem
If the smaller arc of the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 25$ between the points $(-3, 4)$ and $(3, 4)$ is revolved about the given axis, find the area of the resulting surface.
The y-axis.

## Rule Used
Extension of Definition 5.19 for revolution about the y-axis (Eq 5.20)

## Solution
**Step 1 — Understand the symmetry of revolution**
The points $(-3, 4)$ and $(3, 4)$ bound the upper arc of the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 25$. 
Revolving the complete arc from $x = -3$ to $x = 3$ about the y-axis results in the left and right halves tracing out the exact same surface (a spherical cap). 
Thus, to find the surface area, we need only revolve the right half of the arc from $x = 0$ to $x = 3$. We will use integration with respect to $y$, corresponding to $y \in [4, 5]$ (since $y = 5$ at $x = 0$ and $y = 4$ at $x = 3$).

**Step 2 — Set up the function $x = g(y)$ and find $g'(y)$**
$$x^2 + y^2 = 25 \implies x = \sqrt{25 - y^2}$$
$$g'(y) = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{25 - y^2}} \cdot (-2y) = \frac{-y}{\sqrt{25 - y^2}}$$

**Step 3 — Construct the arc length differential factor**
$$1 + [g'(y)]^2 = 1 + \left(\frac{-y}{\sqrt{25 - y^2}}\right)^2 = 1 + \frac{y^2}{25 - y^2} = \frac{25 - y^2 + y^2}{25 - y^2} = \frac{25}{25 - y^2}$$
$$\sqrt{1 + [g'(y)]^2} = \frac{5}{\sqrt{25 - y^2}}$$

**Step 4 — Set up the surface area integral**
The area of a surface of revolution about the y-axis is $S = \int 2\pi x \, ds$.
$$S = \int_{4}^{5} 2\pi (\sqrt{25 - y^2}) \left(\frac{5}{\sqrt{25 - y^2}}\right) dy$$
$$S = \int_{4}^{5} 10\pi dy$$

**Step 5 — Evaluate the integral**
$$S = 10\pi [y]_4^5 = 10\pi(5 - 4) = 10\pi$$

## Answer
$10\pi$

## Check by Differentiation
The integrand simplifies cleanly to a constant $10\pi$, and $\frac{d}{dy}[10\pi y] = 10\pi$. Verified.
