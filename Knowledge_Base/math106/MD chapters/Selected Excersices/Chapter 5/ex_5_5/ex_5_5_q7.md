## Problem
Find the arc length of the graph of the equation from $A$ to $B$.
$y = 5 - \sqrt{x^3}$; $A(1, 4)$, $B(4, -3)$

## Rule Used
Definition 5.14 (Arc length $L_a^b$ with respect to $x$)

## Solution
**Step 1 — Find the derivative $f'(x)$**
Given $f(x) = 5 - x^{3/2}$, the derivative is:
$$f'(x) = -\frac{3}{2}x^{1/2}$$

**Step 2 — Construct the integrand $\sqrt{1 + [f'(x)]^2}$**
$$[f'(x)]^2 = \left(-\frac{3}{2}x^{1/2}\right)^2 = \frac{9}{4}x$$
$$\sqrt{1 + [f'(x)]^2} = \sqrt{1 + \frac{9}{4}x}$$

**Step 3 — Set up the definite integral**
The interval spans from $x = 1$ to $x = 4$.
$$L_1^4 = \int_{1}^{4} \sqrt{1 + \frac{9}{4}x} dx$$

**Step 4 — Evaluate the integral using substitution**
Let $u = 1 + \frac{9}{4}x$.
$$du = \frac{9}{4} dx \implies dx = \frac{4}{9} du$$
Change the limits of integration:
When $x = 1$, $u = 1 + \frac{9}{4}(1) = \frac{13}{4}$.
When $x = 4$, $u = 1 + \frac{9}{4}(4) = 1 + 9 = 10$.
Substitute into the integral:
$$L_1^4 = \int_{13/4}^{10} \sqrt{u} \left( \frac{4}{9} du \right) = \frac{4}{9} \int_{13/4}^{10} u^{1/2} du$$
$$= \frac{4}{9} \left[ \frac{2}{3}u^{3/2} \right]_{13/4}^{10} = \frac{8}{27} \left( 10^{3/2} - \left(\frac{13}{4}\right)^{3/2} \right)$$
$$= \frac{8}{27} \left( 10\sqrt{10} - \frac{13\sqrt{13}}{8} \right) = \frac{80\sqrt{10} - 13\sqrt{13}}{27}$$

## Answer
$\frac{80\sqrt{10} - 13\sqrt{13}}{27}$

## Check by Differentiation
Differentiate the antiderivative $\frac{8}{27} (1 + \frac{9}{4}x)^{3/2}$:
$\frac{d}{dx} \left[ \frac{8}{27} \left( 1 + \frac{9}{4}x \right)^{3/2} \right] = \frac{8}{27} \cdot \frac{3}{2} \left( 1 + \frac{9}{4}x \right)^{1/2} \cdot \left(\frac{9}{4}\right) = \left( 1 + \frac{9}{4}x \right)^{1/2}$. Verified.
