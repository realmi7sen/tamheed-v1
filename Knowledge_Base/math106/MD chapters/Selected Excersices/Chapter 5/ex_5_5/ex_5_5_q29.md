## Problem
The graph of the equation from $A$ to $B$ is revolved about the x-axis. Find the area of the resulting surface.
$4x = y^2$; $A(0, 0)$, $B(1, 2)$

## Rule Used
Definition 5.19 (Surface area $S$ about the x-axis)

## Solution
**Step 1 — Express $y$ as a function of $x$ and find $y'$**
Since $y$ goes from 0 to 2, we take the positive root:
$$y^2 = 4x \implies y = 2\sqrt{x}$$
The derivative is:
$$y' = 2 \cdot \frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}$$

**Step 2 — Construct the arc length differential factor**
$$1 + (y')^2 = 1 + \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}\right)^2 = 1 + \frac{1}{x} = \frac{x+1}{x}$$
$$\sqrt{1 + (y')^2} = \sqrt{\frac{x+1}{x}}$$

**Step 3 — Set up the surface area integral**
The interval for $x$ is from $x = 0$ to $x = 1$.
$$S = \int_{0}^{1} 2\pi y \sqrt{1 + (y')^2} dx = \int_{0}^{1} 2\pi (2\sqrt{x}) \sqrt{\frac{x+1}{x}} dx$$
$$S = \int_{0}^{1} 4\pi \sqrt{x} \frac{\sqrt{x+1}}{\sqrt{x}} dx = \int_{0}^{1} 4\pi \sqrt{x+1} dx$$

**Step 4 — Evaluate the integral**
$$S = 4\pi \int_{0}^{1} (x+1)^{1/2} dx = 4\pi \left[ \frac{2}{3}(x+1)^{3/2} \right]_0^1$$
$$S = \frac{8\pi}{3} \left( (1+1)^{3/2} - (0+1)^{3/2} \right) = \frac{8\pi}{3} \left( 2^{3/2} - 1^{3/2} \right)$$
$$S = \frac{8\pi}{3} (2\sqrt{2} - 1)$$

## Answer
$\frac{8\pi}{3} (2\sqrt{2} - 1)$

## Check by Differentiation
Differentiating $\frac{8\pi}{3} (x+1)^{3/2}$ yields $\frac{8\pi}{3} \cdot \frac{3}{2} (x+1)^{1/2} = 4\pi\sqrt{x+1}$. Verified.
