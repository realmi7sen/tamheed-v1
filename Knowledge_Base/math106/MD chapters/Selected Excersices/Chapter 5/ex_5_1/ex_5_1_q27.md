## Problem
Set up sums of integrals that can be used to find the area of the region bounded by the graphs of the equations by integrating with respect to (a) x and (b) y.
$y = \sqrt{x}$; $y = -x$; $x = 1$; $x = 4$

## Rule Used
Guidelines for Finding the Area of an $R_x$ Region (5.3) and an $R_y$ Region (5.4)

## Solution
**Part (a) — Integrating with respect to x**
**Step 1 — Identify Boundaries:**
The bounds for $x$ are provided directly: $x \in [1, 4]$.
Over this interval, $\sqrt{x}$ is positive and $-x$ is negative.
Upper boundary: $y = \sqrt{x}$. Lower boundary: $y = -x$.
**Step 2 — Construct Integral:**
$$A = \int_{1}^{4} [\sqrt{x} - (-x)] dx = \int_{1}^{4} (\sqrt{x} + x) dx$$

**Part (b) — Integrating with respect to y**
**Step 1 — Solve for x in terms of y:**
$y = \sqrt{x} \Rightarrow x = y^2$ (Applicable for $y \ge 0$)
$y = -x \Rightarrow x = -y$ (Applicable for $y \le 0$)
**Step 2 — Identify minimum and maximum y-values on the region:**
At $x = 4$, the top curve evaluates to $y = \sqrt{4} = 2$.
At $x = 4$, the bottom curve evaluates to $y = -4$.
So, $y$ spans from $-4$ to $2$.
**Step 3 — Break region into intervals along y-axis:**
The right boundary is always the vertical line $x = 4$.
The left boundary changes based on $y$:
- For $y \in [-4, -1]$: Left boundary is $x = -y$.
- For $y \in [-1, 1]$: Left boundary is the vertical line $x = 1$.
- For $y \in [1, 2]$: Left boundary is $x = y^2$.
**Step 4 — Construct Sum of Integrals:**
$$A = \int_{-4}^{-1} [4 - (-y)] dy + \int_{-1}^{1} (4 - 1) dy + \int_{1}^{2} (4 - y^2) dy$$

## Answer
(a) $\int_{1}^{4} (\sqrt{x} + x) dx$
(b) $\int_{-4}^{-1} (4 + y) dy + \int_{-1}^{1} 3 dy + \int_{1}^{2} (4 - y^2) dy$
