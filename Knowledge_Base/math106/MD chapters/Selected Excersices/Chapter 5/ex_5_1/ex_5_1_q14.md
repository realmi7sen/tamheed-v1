## Problem
Sketch the region bounded by the graphs of the equations and find its area: 
$x = y^2$; $x - y = 2$

## Rule Used
Guidelines for Finding the Area of an $R_y$ Region (5.4)

## Solution
**Step 1 — Express equations in terms of $y$**
$x = y^2$
$x = y + 2$

**Step 2 — Find the points of intersection**
Set the equations equal:
$$y^2 = y + 2$$
$$y^2 - y - 2 = 0$$
$$(y - 2)(y + 1) = 0$$
The boundaries in $y$ are $y = -1$ and $y = 2$.

**Step 3 — Identify left and right boundaries**
On $[-1, 2]$, test $y = 0$: $x = 2$ versus $x = 0$. 
Thus, the right boundary is $f(y) = y + 2$ and the left boundary is $g(y) = y^2$.

**Step 4 — Set up the definite integral**
$$A = \int_{-1}^{2} [(y + 2) - y^2] dy = \int_{-1}^{2} (-y^2 + y + 2) dy$$

**Step 5 — Evaluate the integral**
$$A = \left[ -\frac{y^3}{3} + \frac{y^2}{2} + 2y \right]_{-1}^2$$
$$A = \left( -\frac{8}{3} + \frac{4}{2} + 4 \right) - \left( \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{2} - 2 \right)$$
$$A = \left( -\frac{8}{3} + 6 \right) - \left( \frac{5}{6} - \frac{12}{6} \right) = \left( \frac{10}{3} \right) - \left( -\frac{7}{6} \right)$$
$$A = \frac{20}{6} + \frac{7}{6} = \frac{27}{6} = \frac{9}{2}$$

## Answer
$\frac{9}{2}$

## Check by Differentiation
Verify the antiderivative:
$\frac{d}{dy} \left[ -\frac{y^3}{3} + \frac{y^2}{2} + 2y \right] = -y^2 + y + 2$. Verified.
