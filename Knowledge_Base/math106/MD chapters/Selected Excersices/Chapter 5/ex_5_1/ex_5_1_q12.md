## Problem
Sketch the region bounded by the graphs of the equations and find its area: 
$x = y^2$; $y - x = 2$; $y = -2$; $y = 3$

## Rule Used
Guidelines for Finding the Area of an $R_y$ Region (5.4)

## Solution
**Step 1 — Express $x$ in terms of $y$**
$x = y^2$
$x = y - 2$

**Step 2 — Identify boundaries on the given interval**
The interval is given as $y \in [-2, 3]$. 
Test a point, e.g., $y = 0$: $x = 0^2 = 0$ and $x = 0 - 2 = -2$.
Because $0 > -2$, the right boundary is $f(y) = y^2$ and the left boundary is $g(y) = y - 2$.

**Step 3 — Set up the definite integral**
$$A = \int_{-2}^{3} [y^2 - (y - 2)] dy = \int_{-2}^{3} (y^2 - y + 2) dy$$

**Step 4 — Evaluate the integral**
$$A = \left[ \frac{y^3}{3} - \frac{y^2}{2} + 2y \right]_{-2}^3$$
$$A = \left( \frac{27}{3} - \frac{9}{2} + 6 \right) - \left( -\frac{8}{3} - \frac{4}{2} - 4 \right)$$
$$A = \left( 9 - 4.5 + 6 \right) - \left( -\frac{8}{3} - 6 \right)$$
$$A = 10.5 - \left( \frac{-8 - 18}{3} \right) = \frac{21}{2} + \frac{26}{3}$$
$$A = \frac{63}{6} + \frac{52}{6} = \frac{115}{6}$$

## Answer
$\frac{115}{6}$

## Check by Differentiation
Verify the antiderivative:
$\frac{d}{dy} \left[ \frac{y^3}{3} - \frac{y^2}{2} + 2y \right] = y^2 - y + 2$. Verified.
