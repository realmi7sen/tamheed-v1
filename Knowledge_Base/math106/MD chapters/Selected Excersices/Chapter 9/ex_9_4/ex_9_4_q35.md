## Problem
Find the area of the surface generated by revolving the graph of the equation about the polar axis.
$r = 2 + 2 \cos \theta$

## Rule Used
Surfaces of Revolution in Polar Coordinates 9.15

## Solution
**Step 1 — Select bounds for revolution**
To avoid retracing the surface when revolving around the polar axis, we revolve only the top half of the cardioid.
Bounds: $\theta = 0$ to $\theta = \pi$.

**Step 2 — Find the arc length differential $ds$**
$$\frac{dr}{d\theta} = -2 \sin \theta$$
$$r^2 + \left(\frac{dr}{d\theta}\right)^2 = (2 + 2 \cos \theta)^2 + (-2 \sin \theta)^2 = 4 + 8 \cos \theta + 4 \cos^2 \theta + 4 \sin^2 \theta$$
Because $4 \cos^2 \theta + 4 \sin^2 \theta = 4$:
$$r^2 + \left(\frac{dr}{d\theta}\right)^2 = 8 + 8 \cos \theta = 8(1 + \cos \theta)$$
Use the half-angle formula $1 + \cos \theta = 2 \cos^2(\theta/2)$:
$$r^2 + \left(\frac{dr}{d\theta}\right)^2 = 16 \cos^2(\theta/2)$$
$$ds = \sqrt{16 \cos^2(\theta/2)} d\theta = 4 \cos(\theta/2) d\theta$$ (since $\cos(\theta/2) \ge 0$ on $[0, \pi]$).

**Step 3 — Set up the surface area integral**
$$S = \int_{0}^{\pi} 2\pi y ds = \int_{0}^{\pi} 2\pi (r \sin \theta) ds$$
Substitute $r = 2 + 2 \cos \theta = 4 \cos^2(\theta/2)$ and $\sin \theta = 2 \sin(\theta/2)\cos(\theta/2)$:
$$S = \int_{0}^{\pi} 2\pi \left( 4 \cos^2(\theta/2) \cdot 2 \sin(\theta/2)\cos(\theta/2) \right) \left( 4 \cos(\theta/2) \right) d\theta$$
$$S = \int_{0}^{\pi} 64\pi \cos^4(\theta/2) \sin(\theta/2) d\theta$$

**Step 4 — Evaluate the integral**
Let $u = \cos(\theta/2)$. Then $du = -\frac{1}{2} \sin(\theta/2) d\theta \implies -2du = \sin(\theta/2) d\theta$.
Limits: $\theta = 0 \implies u = 1$; $\theta = \pi \implies u = 0$.
$$S = \int_{1}^{0} 64\pi u^4 (-2du) = -128\pi \int_{1}^{0} u^4 du = 128\pi \int_{0}^{1} u^4 du$$
$$S = 128\pi \left[ \frac{u^5}{5} \right]_0^1 = \frac{128\pi}{5}$$

## Answer
$\frac{128\pi}{5}$

## Check by Differentiation
$\frac{d}{du} \left[ 128\pi \frac{u^5}{5} \right] = 128\pi u^4$. Substituting back $u = \cos(\theta/2)$ and multiplying by $du/d\theta$ verifies the integrand. Verified.
