## Problem
Find the area of the region bounded by the graph of the polar equation.
$r = 5 \sin \theta$

## Rule Used
Theorem 9.12 (Area of a Polar Region)

## Solution
**Step 1 — Determine the limits of integration**
The graph of $r = 5 \sin \theta$ is a circle resting on the polar axis. It is traced exactly once as $\theta$ goes from $0$ to $\pi$. Thus, $\alpha = 0$ and $\beta = \pi$.

**Step 2 — Set up the integral**
$$A = \int_{0}^{\pi} \frac{1}{2} (5 \sin \theta)^2 d\theta$$
$$A = \int_{0}^{\pi} \frac{25}{2} \sin^2 \theta d\theta$$

**Step 3 — Apply trigonometric identities and evaluate**
Use the half-angle identity $\sin^2 \theta = \frac{1 - \cos 2\theta}{2}$:
$$A = \frac{25}{2} \int_{0}^{\pi} \left( \frac{1 - \cos 2\theta}{2} \right) d\theta = \frac{25}{4} \int_{0}^{\pi} (1 - \cos 2\theta) d\theta$$
$$A = \frac{25}{4} \left[ \theta - \frac{1}{2} \sin 2\theta \right]_0^\pi$$
$$A = \frac{25}{4} \left( \pi - \frac{1}{2} \sin 2\pi \right) - \frac{25}{4} (0)$$
$$A = \frac{25\pi}{4}$$

## Answer
$\frac{25\pi}{4}$

## Check by Differentiation
Convert to rectangular coordinates: $r^2 = 5r \sin \theta \implies x^2 + y^2 = 5y \implies x^2 + (y - 5/2)^2 = 25/4$. This is a circle of radius $5/2$. The area is $\pi (5/2)^2 = 25\pi/4$. Verified.
