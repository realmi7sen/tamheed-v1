## Problem
Find the area of the region bounded by one loop of the graph of the polar equation.
$r^2 = 4 \cos 2\theta$

## Rule Used
Theorem 9.12 (Area of a Polar Region)

## Solution
**Step 1 — Determine the limits for one loop**
The equation is a lemniscate. $r^2$ must be non-negative, meaning $\cos 2\theta \ge 0$.
The right loop occurs when $2\theta$ is between $-\pi/2$ and $\pi/2$, which translates to $\theta$ between $-\pi/4$ and $\pi/4$.
By symmetry, we can calculate the area of the top half of the loop (from $0$ to $\pi/4$) and double the result.

**Step 2 — Set up the integral**
$$A_{\text{one loop}} = 2 \int_{0}^{\pi/4} \frac{1}{2} r^2 d\theta$$
Notice the formula requires $r^2$, and our equation provides exactly $r^2$.
$$A_{\text{one loop}} = \int_{0}^{\pi/4} (4 \cos 2\theta) d\theta$$

**Step 3 — Evaluate the integral**
$$A_{\text{one loop}} = \left[ 4 \left( \frac{1}{2} \sin 2\theta \right) \right]_0^{\pi/4}$$
$$A_{\text{one loop}} = \left[ 2 \sin 2\theta \right]_0^{\pi/4}$$
$$A_{\text{one loop}} = 2 \sin(\pi/2) - 2 \sin(0)$$
$$A_{\text{one loop}} = 2(1) - 0 = 2$$

## Answer
$2$
