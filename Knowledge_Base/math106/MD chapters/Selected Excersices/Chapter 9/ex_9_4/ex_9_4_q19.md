## Problem
Find the area of the region that is outside the graph of the first equation and inside the graph of the second equation.
$r = 2 + 2 \cos \theta$, $r = 3$

## Rule Used
Area between Two Polar Curves

## Solution
**Step 1 — Find the points of intersection**
Set the two equations equal to each other to find where the curves cross.
$$2 + 2 \cos \theta = 3$$
$$2 \cos \theta = 1$$
$$\cos \theta = 1/2$$
$$\theta = -\pi/3, \pi/3$$
The region *outside* the cardioid $r = 2 + 2\cos\theta$ and *inside* the circle $r=3$ occurs to the left of these intersections. Wait, evaluate the region carefully: 
At $\theta = \pi$, cardioid $r = 0$, circle $r = 3$. The circle is "outside" the cardioid for angles between $\pi/3$ and $5\pi/3$. By symmetry, we can integrate from $\pi/3$ to $\pi$ and double the result.

**Step 2 — Set up the integral**
Outer curve: $r_{\text{outer}} = 3$
Inner curve: $r_{\text{inner}} = 2 + 2 \cos \theta$
$$A = 2 \left( \int_{\pi/3}^{\pi} \frac{1}{2} \left[ (3)^2 - (2 + 2 \cos \theta)^2 \right] d\theta \right)$$
$$A = \int_{\pi/3}^{\pi} [9 - (4 + 8 \cos \theta + 4 \cos^2 \theta)] d\theta$$
$$A = \int_{\pi/3}^{\pi} (5 - 8 \cos \theta - 4 \cos^2 \theta) d\theta$$

**Step 3 — Use trigonometric identities**
Substitute $\cos^2 \theta = \frac{1 + \cos 2\theta}{2}$:
$$A = \int_{\pi/3}^{\pi} \left( 5 - 8 \cos \theta - 4 \left( \frac{1 + \cos 2\theta}{2} \right) \right) d\theta$$
$$A = \int_{\pi/3}^{\pi} (5 - 8 \cos \theta - 2 - 2 \cos 2\theta) d\theta$$
$$A = \int_{\pi/3}^{\pi} (3 - 8 \cos \theta - 2 \cos 2\theta) d\theta$$

**Step 4 — Evaluate the integral**
$$A = \left[ 3\theta - 8 \sin \theta - \sin 2\theta \right]_{\pi/3}^\pi$$
$$A = (3\pi - 0 - 0) - \left( 3(\pi/3) - 8 \sin(\pi/3) - \sin(2\pi/3) \right)$$
$$A = 3\pi - \left( \pi - 8\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right) - \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \right)$$
$$A = 3\pi - \pi + 4\sqrt{3} + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 2\pi + \frac{9\sqrt{3}}{2}$$

## Answer
$2\pi + \frac{9\sqrt{3}}{2}$
