## Problem
Find the slopes of the tangent line and the normal line at the point on the curve that corresponds to $t = 1$.
$x = 4t^2 - 5, y = 2t + 3$; $t$ in $\mathbb{R}$

## Rule Used
Theorem 9.4 (Derivative of Parametric Equations)

## Solution
**Step 1 — Find $dy/dt$ and $dx/dt$**
$$\frac{dx}{dt} = 8t$$
$$\frac{dy}{dt} = 2$$

**Step 2 — Find the slope of the tangent line $dy/dx$ at $t = 1$**
Using Theorem 9.4:
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt} = \frac{2}{8t} = \frac{1}{4t}$$
Evaluate at $t = 1$:
$$m_{\text{tangent}} = \frac{1}{4(1)} = \frac{1}{4}$$

**Step 3 — Find the slope of the normal line**
The normal line's slope is the negative reciprocal:
$$m_{\text{normal}} = -4$$

## Answer
Tangent slope: $\frac{1}{4}$
Normal slope: $-4$

## Check by Differentiation
Eliminate parameter: $t = \frac{y-3}{2}$, so $x = (y-3)^2 - 5$. Differentiating implicitly gives $1 = 2(y-3)y'$. At $t=1$, $y = 5$. Thus $1 = 2(5-3)y' \implies y' = \frac{1}{4}$. Verified.
