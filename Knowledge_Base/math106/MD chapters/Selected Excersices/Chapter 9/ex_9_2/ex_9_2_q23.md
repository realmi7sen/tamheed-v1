## Problem
Find the length of the curve.
$x = e^t \cos t, y = e^t \sin t$; $0 \le t \le \pi/2$

## Rule Used
Theorem 9.6 (Arc length of parametric curve)

## Solution
**Step 1 — Find the derivatives using the product rule**
$$\frac{dx}{dt} = e^t \cos t - e^t \sin t = e^t(\cos t - \sin t)$$
$$\frac{dy}{dt} = e^t \sin t + e^t \cos t = e^t(\sin t + \cos t)$$

**Step 2 — Square the derivatives and add them**
$$(\frac{dx}{dt})^2 = e^{2t}(\cos^2 t - 2\cos t \sin t + \sin^2 t) = e^{2t}(1 - 2\cos t \sin t)$$
$$(\frac{dy}{dt})^2 = e^{2t}(\sin^2 t + 2\sin t \cos t + \cos^2 t) = e^{2t}(1 + 2\sin t \cos t)$$
Summing them up:
$$(\frac{dx}{dt})^2 + (\frac{dy}{dt})^2 = e^{2t}(1 - 2\cos t \sin t + 1 + 2\sin t \cos t) = 2e^{2t}$$

**Step 3 — Set up the arc length integral**
$$L = \int_{0}^{\pi/2} \sqrt{2e^{2t}} dt = \int_{0}^{\pi/2} \sqrt{2} e^t dt$$

**Step 4 — Evaluate the integral**
$$L = \sqrt{2} \left[ e^t \right]_0^{\pi/2}$$
$$L = \sqrt{2}(e^{\pi/2} - e^0) = \sqrt{2}(e^{\pi/2} - 1)$$

## Answer
$\sqrt{2}(e^{\pi/2} - 1)$

## Check by Differentiation
$\frac{d}{dt} \left[ \sqrt{2} e^t \right] = \sqrt{2} e^t$. Verified.
