## Problem
Sketch the graph of the polar equation.
$r = 3 \cos \theta$

## Rule Used
Relationships between Rectangular and Polar Coordinates 9.9

## Solution
**Step 1 — Convert to rectangular coordinates**
Multiply both sides of the equation by $r$:
$$r^2 = 3r \cos \theta$$

**Step 2 — Substitute rectangular equivalents**
Using $r^2 = x^2 + y^2$ and $x = r \cos \theta$:
$$x^2 + y^2 = 3x$$

**Step 3 — Complete the square**
Subtract $3x$ from both sides and group the $x$ terms:
$$x^2 - 3x + y^2 = 0$$
Add $(3/2)^2 = 9/4$ to both sides to complete the square:
$$\left(x^2 - 3x + \frac{9}{4}\right) + y^2 = \frac{9}{4}$$
$$\left(x - \frac{3}{2}\right)^2 + y^2 = \left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^2$$

**Step 4 — Identify the shape and sketch**
The equation represents a circle with radius $3/2$, centered at $(3/2, 0)$ on the positive x-axis (polar axis). The circle is tangent to the y-axis at the pole.

## Answer
The graph is a circle centered at $(3/2, 0)$ with a radius of $3/2$.
