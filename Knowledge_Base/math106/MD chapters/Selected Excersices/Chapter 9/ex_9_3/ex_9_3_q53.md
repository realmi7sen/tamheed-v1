## Problem
Find the slope of the tangent line to the graph of the polar equation at the point corresponding to the given value of $\theta$.
$r = 5 + 3 \sin \theta; \quad \theta = \pi/4$

## Rule Used
Theorem 9.11 (Slope of tangent to a polar curve)

## Solution
**Step 1 — Find $r$ and $dr/d\theta$ at the given angle**
Given $r = 5 + 3 \sin \theta$, calculate the derivative:
$$\frac{dr}{d\theta} = 3 \cos \theta$$

At $\theta = \pi/4$, $\sin(\pi/4) = \cos(\pi/4) = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$.
$$r = 5 + 3\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right) = 5 + \frac{3\sqrt{2}}{2}$$
$$\frac{dr}{d\theta} = 3\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right) = \frac{3\sqrt{2}}{2}$$

**Step 2 — Apply the slope formula**
$$m = \frac{\frac{dr}{d\theta} \sin \theta + r \cos \theta}{\frac{dr}{d\theta} \cos \theta - r \sin \theta}$$
Since $\sin(\pi/4) = \cos(\pi/4)$, we can factor out and cancel the trigonometric terms from the numerator and denominator:
$$m = \frac{\frac{dr}{d\theta} + r}{\frac{dr}{d\theta} - r}$$

**Step 3 — Substitute the evaluated quantities and simplify**
$$m = \frac{\frac{3\sqrt{2}}{2} + \left(5 + \frac{3\sqrt{2}}{2}\right)}{\frac{3\sqrt{2}}{2} - \left(5 + \frac{3\sqrt{2}}{2}\right)}$$
$$m = \frac{5 + \frac{6\sqrt{2}}{2}}{-5}$$
$$m = \frac{5 + 3\sqrt{2}}{-5}$$
$$m = -1 - \frac{3\sqrt{2}}{5}$$

## Answer
$-1 - \frac{3\sqrt{2}}{5}$
