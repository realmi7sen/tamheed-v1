## Problem
Sketch the graph of the polar equation.
$r = 2 + 4 \sin \theta$

## Rule Used
Graph of a Polar Equation / Symmetry Tests

## Solution
**Step 1 — Test for symmetry**
Substituting $\pi - \theta$ for $\theta$:
$$r = 2 + 4 \sin(\pi - \theta) = 2 + 4 \sin \theta$$
The graph is symmetric with respect to the vertical line $\theta = \pi/2$.

**Step 2 — Identify the shape**
The equation is of the form $r = a + b \sin \theta$. Since $|a| < |b|$ ($2 < 4$), the graph is a limaçon with an inner loop.

**Step 3 — Find key points and intercepts**
- $\theta = 0 \implies r = 2 \quad \rightarrow (2, 0)$
- $\theta = \pi/2 \implies r = 6 \quad \rightarrow (6, \pi/2)$
- $\theta = \pi \implies r = 2 \quad \rightarrow (2, \pi)$
- $\theta = 3\pi/2 \implies r = 2 - 4 = -2 \quad \rightarrow (-2, 3\pi/2)$ (which maps to $(2, \pi/2)$).

To find where the loop begins and ends, set $r = 0$:
$$2 + 4 \sin \theta = 0 \implies \sin \theta = -1/2$$
$$\theta = 7\pi/6, 11\pi/6$$
Between these angles, $r$ is negative, which traces out the inner loop in the first and second quadrants.

## Answer
The graph is a limaçon oriented vertically along the y-axis, featuring a large outer loop extending to $r=6$ at $\theta=\pi/2$, and a smaller inner loop created as $r$ becomes negative.
