## Problem
Find the slope of the tangent line to the graph of the polar equation at the point corresponding to the given value of $\theta$.
$r = 2 \cos \theta; \quad \theta = \pi/3$

## Rule Used
Theorem 9.11 (Slope of tangent to a polar curve)

## Solution
**Step 1 — Find $r$ and $dr/d\theta$ at the given angle**
Given $r = 2 \cos \theta$, calculate the derivative:
$$\frac{dr}{d\theta} = -2 \sin \theta$$

At $\theta = \pi/3$:
$$r = 2 \cos(\pi/3) = 2(1/2) = 1$$
$$\frac{dr}{d\theta} = -2 \sin(\pi/3) = -2\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right) = -\sqrt{3}$$

**Step 2 — Apply the slope formula**
The slope $m$ is given by:
$$m = \frac{\frac{dr}{d\theta} \sin \theta + r \cos \theta}{\frac{dr}{d\theta} \cos \theta - r \sin \theta}$$
Substitute the evaluated values:
$$m = \frac{(-\sqrt{3})(\sqrt{3}/2) + (1)(1/2)}{(-\sqrt{3})(1/2) - (1)(\sqrt{3}/2)}$$

**Step 3 — Simplify the expression**
$$m = \frac{-3/2 + 1/2}{-\sqrt{3}/2 - \sqrt{3}/2}$$
$$m = \frac{-1}{-\sqrt{3}}$$
$$m = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$$

## Answer
$\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$

## Check by Differentiation
Convert $r = 2\cos\theta$ to rectangular coordinates: $x^2 + y^2 = 2x$, which is a circle $(x-1)^2 + y^2 = 1$. Differentiating implicitly yields $2(x-1) + 2y y' = 0 \implies y' = -(x-1)/y$.
At $\theta=\pi/3$, $x = 1 \cos(\pi/3) = 1/2$ and $y = 1 \sin(\pi/3) = \sqrt{3}/2$.
$y' = -(1/2 - 1)/(\sqrt{3}/2) = (1/2)/(\sqrt{3}/2) = 1/\sqrt{3} = \sqrt{3}/3$. Verified.
