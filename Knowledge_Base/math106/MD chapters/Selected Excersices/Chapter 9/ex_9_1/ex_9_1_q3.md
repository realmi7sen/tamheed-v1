## Problem
(a) Find an equation in $x$ and $y$ whose graph contains the points on the curve $C$. 
(b) Sketch the graph of $C$ and indicate the orientation.
$x = t^2 + 1$, $y = t^2 - 1$; $-2 \le t \le 2$

## Rule Used
Eliminating the parameter

## Solution
**Step 1 — Isolate $t^2$**
From the first parametric equation, solve for $t^2$:
$$x = t^2 + 1 \implies t^2 = x - 1$$

**Step 2 — Substitute $t^2$ into the $y$ equation**
Substitute $t^2 = x - 1$ into the second parametric equation:
$$y = (x - 1) - 1$$
$$y = x - 2$$

**Step 3 — Determine the domain and track the orientation**
Analyze the behavior of $x$ over the interval $-2 \le t \le 2$:
- At $t = -2$, $x = (-2)^2 + 1 = 5$ and $y = (-2)^2 - 1 = 3$. Point: $(5, 3)$.
- As $t$ increases from $-2$ to $0$, $t^2$ decreases from $4$ to $0$. At $t=0$, $x = 1$ and $y = -1$. Point: $(1, -1)$.
- As $t$ increases from $0$ to $2$, $t^2$ increases from $0$ to $4$. At $t=2$, $x = 5$ and $y = 3$. Point: $(5, 3)$.

The lowest possible value for $t^2$ is 0, which means the lowest possible value for $x$ is 1. Thus, the curve traverses the line segment from $(5, 3)$ down to $(1, -1)$, and then retraces its path back up to $(5, 3)$.

## Answer
(a) $y = x - 2$
(b) The graph is a line segment bound between $(1, -1)$ and $(5, 3)$. The orientation begins at $(5, 3)$, moves down and left to $(1, -1)$, and then reverses direction to end back at $(5, 3)$.

## Check by Differentiation
Substitute $x(t)$ into the derived Cartesian equation: 
$x - 2 = (t^2 + 1) - 2 = t^2 - 1$. This exactly matches $y(t)$. Verified.
