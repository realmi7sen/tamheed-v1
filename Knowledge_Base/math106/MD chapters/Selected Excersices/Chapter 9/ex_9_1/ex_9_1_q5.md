## Problem
(a) Find an equation in $x$ and $y$ whose graph contains the points on the curve $C$. 
(b) Sketch the graph of $C$ and indicate the orientation.
$x = 4t^2 - 5$, $y = 2t + 3$; $t$ in $\mathbb{R}$

## Rule Used
Eliminating the parameter

## Solution
**Step 1 — Solve for $t$ in terms of $y$**
It is easier to isolate $t$ from the linear equation for $y$:
$$y = 2t + 3 \implies 2t = y - 3 \implies t = \frac{y - 3}{2}$$

**Step 2 — Substitute $t$ into the $x$ equation**
Substitute $t = \frac{y - 3}{2}$ into the first equation:
$$x = 4\left(\frac{y - 3}{2}\right)^2 - 5$$
$$x = 4\left(\frac{(y - 3)^2}{4}\right) - 5$$
$$x = (y - 3)^2 - 5$$

**Step 3 — Expand the Cartesian equation**
$$x = y^2 - 6y + 9 - 5$$
$$x = y^2 - 6y + 4$$

**Step 4 — Determine the domain and orientation**
Because $t \in \mathbb{R}$, $y = 2t + 3$ can take any real value ($y \in \mathbb{R}$). 
The curve is a complete parabola opening to the right with its vertex at $(-5, 3)$.
Since $y = 2t + 3$, as $t$ goes from $-\infty$ to $\infty$, $y$ strictly increases from $-\infty$ to $\infty$. The orientation flows upward along the entire parabola.

## Answer
(a) $x = (y - 3)^2 - 5$ (or $x = y^2 - 6y + 4$)
(b) The graph is a parabola opening to the right with vertex at $(-5, 3)$. The orientation traces the curve continuously from bottom to top as $t$ increases.

## Check by Differentiation
Substitute $y(t)$ back into the Cartesian equation:
$x = (2t + 3 - 3)^2 - 5 = (2t)^2 - 5 = 4t^2 - 5$. This matches $x(t)$. Verified.
