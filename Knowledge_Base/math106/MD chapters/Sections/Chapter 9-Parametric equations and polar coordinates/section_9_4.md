## Theorems
* **Theorem 9.12**
  If a function $f$ is continuous and $f(\theta) \ge 0$ on the interval $[\alpha, \beta]$, where $0 \le \alpha < \beta \le 2\pi$, then the area $A$ of the region bounded by the graphs of $r = f(\theta)$, $\theta = \alpha$, and $\theta = \beta$ is given by[cite: 8]:
  $$A = \int_{\alpha}^{\beta} \frac{1}{2} [f(\theta)]^2 d\theta = \int_{\alpha}^{\beta} \frac{1}{2} r^2 d\theta$$

## Formulas
* **Area between Two Polar Curves**
  The area $A$ of a region $R$ bounded by two polar curves $r = f(\theta)$ (outer) and $r = g(\theta)$ (inner), and the lines $\theta = \alpha$ and $\theta = \beta$, is[cite: 8]:
  $$A = \int_{\alpha}^{\beta} \frac{1}{2} [f(\theta)]^2 d\theta - \int_{\alpha}^{\beta} \frac{1}{2} [g(\theta)]^2 d\theta$$

* **Differential of Arc Length in Polar Coordinates 9.14**
  The differential of arc length $ds$ in polar coordinates is[cite: 8]:
  $$ds = \sqrt{r^2 + \left(\frac{dr}{d\theta}\right)^2} d\theta$$
  The arc length $L$ of a curve $r = f(\theta)$ from $\alpha$ to $\beta$ is $L = \int_{\alpha}^{\beta} ds$[cite: 8].

* **Surfaces of Revolution in Polar Coordinates 9.15**
  Let $C$ be the graph of a polar equation $r = f(\theta)$ for $\alpha \le \theta \le \beta$. The surface area $S$ generated by revolving $C$ is[cite: 8]:
  *   **About the polar axis:**
      $$S = \int_{\alpha}^{\beta} 2\pi y \, ds = \int_{\alpha}^{\beta} 2\pi r \sin \theta \, ds$$
  *   **About the line $\theta = \pi/2$ (y-axis):**
      $$S = \int_{\alpha}^{\beta} 2\pi x \, ds = \int_{\alpha}^{\beta} 2\pi r \cos \theta \, ds$$

## Guidelines / Methods
* **Guidelines for Finding the Area of an $R_\theta$ Region 9.13**[cite: 8]
  1. Sketch the region, labeling the graph of $r = f(\theta)$. Find the smallest value $\theta = \alpha$ and the largest value $\theta = \beta$ for points $(r, \theta)$ in the region[cite: 8].
  2. Sketch a typical circular sector and label its central angle $d\theta$[cite: 8].
  3. Express the area of the sector in guideline (2) as $\frac{1}{2}r^2 d\theta$[cite: 8].
  4. Apply the limit of sums operator $\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}$ to the expression in guideline (3) and evaluate the integral[cite: 8].

## Worked Examples
### Example 1
Find the area of the region bounded by the cardioid $r = 2 + 2 \cos \theta$[cite: 8].

**Solution**
* **Step 1:** To use symmetry, find the area of the top half by integrating from $\alpha = 0$ to $\beta = \pi$ and then multiply by 2[cite: 8].
* **Step 2:** The radius of the circular sector is $r = 2 + 2 \cos \theta$[cite: 8].
* **Step 3:** The area of a typical sector is $\frac{1}{2}(2 + 2 \cos \theta)^2 d\theta$[cite: 8].
* **Step 4:** Integrate and evaluate[cite: 8]:
  $$A = 2 \int_{0}^{\pi} \frac{1}{2} (2 + 2 \cos \theta)^2 d\theta = \int_{0}^{\pi} (4 + 8 \cos \theta + 4 \cos^2 \theta) d\theta$$[cite: 8]
  Use the half-angle identity $\cos^2 \theta = \frac{1}{2}(1 + \cos 2\theta)$[cite: 8]:
  $$A = \int_{0}^{\pi} (6 + 8 \cos \theta + 2 \cos 2\theta) d\theta = \left[ 6\theta + 8 \sin \theta + \sin 2\theta \right]_0^\pi = 6\pi$$[cite: 8]

### Example 2
Find the area $A$ of the region $R$ that is inside the cardioid $r = 2 + 2 \cos \theta$ and outside the circle $r = 3$[cite: 8].

**Solution**
* **Step 1:** Find intersection points by setting $2 + 2 \cos \theta = 3$, which gives $\cos \theta = 1/2$. Thus, $\theta = \pi/3$ and $\theta = -\pi/3$[cite: 8].
* **Step 2:** By symmetry, evaluate the top half of the region ($\alpha = 0$ to $\beta = \pi/3$) and double the result[cite: 8].
* **Step 3:** Subtract the inner area from the outer area[cite: 8]:
  $$A = 2 \left[ \int_{0}^{\pi/3} \frac{1}{2} (2 + 2 \cos \theta)^2 d\theta - \int_{0}^{\pi/3} \frac{1}{2} (3)^2 d\theta \right]$$[cite: 8]
  $$A = \int_{0}^{\pi/3} (4 \cos^2 \theta + 8 \cos \theta - 5) d\theta$$[cite: 8]
* **Step 4:** Evaluate using the half-angle identity[cite: 8]:
  $$A = \frac{9}{2}\sqrt{3} - \pi \approx 4.65$$[cite: 8]

### Example 3
Find the length of the cardioid $r = 1 + \cos \theta$[cite: 8].

**Solution**
* **Step 1:** By symmetry, find the length of the top half ($0$ to $\pi$) and double the result[cite: 8].
* **Step 2:** Calculate the arc length differential $ds$[cite: 8].
  $$ds = \sqrt{(1 + \cos \theta)^2 + (-\sin \theta)^2} d\theta = \sqrt{1 + 2 \cos \theta + \cos^2 \theta + \sin^2 \theta} d\theta$$[cite: 8]
  $$ds = \sqrt{2 + 2 \cos \theta} d\theta = \sqrt{2}\sqrt{1 + \cos \theta} d\theta$$[cite: 8]
* **Step 3:** Use the half-angle identity $1 + \cos \theta = 2 \cos^2(\theta/2)$[cite: 8]. Because $\theta \in [0, \pi]$, $\cos(\theta/2)$ is positive, so $\sqrt{\cos^2(\theta/2)} = \cos(\theta/2)$[cite: 8].
  $$ds = \sqrt{2} \sqrt{2 \cos^2(\theta/2)} d\theta = 2 \cos(\theta/2) d\theta$$[cite: 8]
* **Step 4:** Integrate[cite: 8]:
  $$L = 2 \int_{0}^{\pi} 2 \cos(\theta/2) d\theta = 4 \int_{0}^{\pi} \cos(\theta/2) d\theta = 8 \left[ \sin(\theta/2) \right]_0^\pi = 8$$[cite: 8]

### Example 4
The part of the spiral $r = e^{\theta/2}$ from $\theta = 0$ to $\theta = \pi$ is revolved about the polar axis[cite: 8]. Find the area of the resulting surface[cite: 8].

**Solution**
* **Step 1:** Calculate the arc length differential $ds$[cite: 8].
  $$ds = \sqrt{(e^{\theta/2})^2 + \left(\frac{1}{2}e^{\theta/2}\right)^2} d\theta = \sqrt{\frac{5}{4}e^\theta} d\theta = \frac{\sqrt{5}}{2}e^{\theta/2} d\theta$$[cite: 8]
* **Step 2:** Apply formula (9.15) for revolution about the polar axis[cite: 8].
  $$S = \int_{0}^{\pi} 2\pi (r \sin \theta) ds = \int_{0}^{\pi} 2\pi e^{\theta/2} \sin \theta \left(\frac{\sqrt{5}}{2}e^{\theta/2}\right) d\theta = \sqrt{5}\pi \int_{0}^{\pi} e^\theta \sin \theta d\theta$$[cite: 8]
* **Step 3:** Evaluate using integration by parts[cite: 8].
  $$S = \frac{\sqrt{5}\pi}{2} \left[ e^\theta (\sin \theta - \cos \theta) \right]_0^\pi = \frac{\sqrt{5}\pi}{2}(e^\pi + 1) \approx 84.8$$[cite: 8]

### Example 5
An industrial plant releases discharge through a 10 cm circular pipe ($r=5$). A valve disk of the same size ($r=10\cos\theta$) restricts flow[cite: 8]. What percentage of the maximum flow occurs when the center of the valve disk is 5 cm from the center of the pipe?[cite: 8]

**Solution**
* **Step 1:** The total pipe area is $25\pi$[cite: 8]. The area of the opening consists of the left semicircle of the pipe plus two symmetric regions in quadrants I and IV[cite: 8].
* **Step 2:** Find the intersection point in quadrant I[cite: 8].
  $$5 = 10 \cos \theta \implies \cos \theta = 1/2 \implies \theta = \pi/3$$[cite: 8]
* **Step 3:** The area of the region in quadrant I is the area between the pipe and the valve for $\theta$ from $\pi/3$ to $\pi/2$[cite: 8].
  $$A_1 = \int_{\pi/3}^{\pi/2} \frac{1}{2} \left[ 5^2 - (10 \cos \theta)^2 \right] d\theta = \frac{1}{2} \int_{\pi/3}^{\pi/2} [25 - 50(1 + \cos 2\theta)] d\theta$$[cite: 8]
  $$A_1 = \frac{1}{2} [-25\theta - 25 \sin 2\theta]_{\pi/3}^{\pi/2} = \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{25\sqrt{3}}{2} - \frac{25\pi}{6} \right)$$[cite: 8]
* **Step 4:** Calculate total open area and the percentage[cite: 8].
  $$\text{Total Area} = \frac{25\pi}{2} + 2(A_1) = 25 \left( \frac{\pi}{3} + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \right)$$[cite: 8]
  $$\text{Percentage} = \frac{25(\pi/3 + \sqrt{3}/2)}{25\pi} = \frac{1}{3} + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2\pi} \approx 0.61$$[cite: 8]
* **Answer:** About 61% of the maximum flow[cite: 8].
