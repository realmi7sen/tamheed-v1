## Definitions
* **Definition 5.5**
  Assume that $f$ is continuous on the interval $[a, b]$, and let $R$ represent the region bounded by the graph of $f$, the x-axis, and the vertical lines $x = a$ and $x = b$[cite: 2]. The volume $V$ of the solid of revolution produced by revolving region $R$ about the x-axis is given by[cite: 2]:
  $$V = \lim_{||P||\to 0} \sum_k \pi [f(w_k)]^2 \Delta x_k = \int_{a}^{b} \pi [f(x)]^2 dx$$

* **Definition 5.6**
  Assume that $g$ is continuous on the interval $[c, d]$, and let $R_y$ represent the region bounded by the graph of $x = g(y)$, the y-axis, and the horizontal lines $y = c$ and $y = d$[cite: 2]. The volume $V$ of the solid of revolution produced by revolving region $R_y$ about the y-axis is given by[cite: 2]:
  $$V = \lim_{||P||\to 0} \sum_k \pi [g(w_k)]^2 \Delta y_k = \int_{c}^{d} \pi [g(y)]^2 dy$$

## Formulas
* **Volume $V$ of a Circular Disk 5.7**
  To find the volume of a single circular disk, use the formula[cite: 2]:
  $$V = \pi (\text{radius})^2 (\text{thickness})$$

* **Volume $V$ of a Washer 5.9**
  When finding the volume of a washer-shaped solid (a disk with a central hole), the volume is found using the difference of the squares of the radii[cite: 2]:
  $$V = \pi [(\text{outer radius})^2 - (\text{inner radius})^2] \cdot (\text{thickness})$$

## Guidelines / Methods
* **Guidelines for Finding the Volume of a Solid of Revolution Using Disks 5.8**[cite: 2]
  1. Sketch the region $R$ to be revolved, and label the boundaries[cite: 2]. Show a typical vertical rectangle of width $dx$ or a horizontal rectangle of width $dy$[cite: 2].
  2. Sketch the solid generated by $R$ and the disk generated by the rectangle in guideline (1)[cite: 2].
  3. Express the radius of the disk in terms of $x$ or $y$, depending on whether its thickness is $dx$ or $dy$[cite: 2].
  4. Use (5.7) to find a formula for the volume of the disk[cite: 2].
  5. Apply the limit of sums operator $\int_a^b$ or $\int_c^d$ to the expression in guideline (4) and evaluate the integral[cite: 2].

## Worked Examples
### Example 1
The region bounded by the x-axis, the graph of the equation $y = x^2 + 1$, and the lines $x = -1$ and $x = 1$ is revolved about the x-axis[cite: 2]. Find the volume of the resulting solid[cite: 2].

**Solution**
* **Step 1:** Sketch the region and a vertical rectangle with width $dx$[cite: 2]. 
* **Step 2:** Note the properties of the disk[cite: 2]:
  * thickness of disk: $dx$
  * radius of disk: $x^2 + 1$
  * volume of disk: $\pi(x^2 + 1)^2 dx$
* **Step 3:** Setup and evaluate the integral, utilizing symmetry across the y-axis[cite: 2]:
  $$V = \int_{-1}^{1} \pi (x^2 + 1)^2 dx = 2 \int_{0}^{1} \pi (x^4 + 2x^3 + 1) dx$$
  $$= 2\pi \left[ \frac{x^5}{5} + 2\left(\frac{x^3}{3}\right) + x \right]_0^1$$
  $$= 2\pi \left( \frac{1}{5} + \frac{2}{3} + 1 \right) = \frac{56}{15}\pi \approx 11.7$$[cite: 2]

### Example 2
The region bounded by the y-axis and the graphs of $y = x^3$, $y = 1$, and $y = 8$ is revolved about the y-axis[cite: 2]. Find the volume of the resulting solid[cite: 2].

**Solution**
* **Step 1:** Because the axis of revolution is the y-axis, we integrate with respect to $y$[cite: 2]. Solve $y = x^3$ for $x$ to get $x = y^{1/3}$[cite: 2].
* **Step 2:** Note the properties of the disk[cite: 2]:
  * thickness of disk: $dy$
  * radius of disk: $y^{1/3}$
  * volume of disk: $\pi(y^{1/3})^2 dy$
* **Step 3:** Apply the integral from $y = 1$ to $y = 8$[cite: 2]:
  $$V = \int_{1}^{8} \pi(y^{1/3})^2 dy = \pi \int_{1}^{8} y^{2/3} dy = \pi \left[ \frac{y^{5/3}}{5/3} \right]_1^8$$
  $$= \frac{3}{5}\pi [y^{5/3}]_1^8 = \frac{3}{5}\pi [32 - 1] = \frac{93}{5}\pi \approx 58.4$$[cite: 2]

### Example 3
The region bounded by the graphs of the equations $x^2 = y - 2$ and $2y - x - 2 = 0$ and the vertical lines $x = 0$ and $x = 1$ is revolved about the x-axis[cite: 2]. Find the volume of the resulting solid[cite: 2].

**Solution**
* **Step 1:** Solve the equations for $y$ in terms of $x$ to integrate with respect to $x$: $y = x^2 + 2$ and $y = \frac{1}{2}x + 1$[cite: 2].
* **Step 2:** We have a solid with a hole, so use the washer method[cite: 2]:
  * thickness of washer: $dx$
  * outer radius: $x^2 + 2$
  * inner radius: $\frac{1}{2}x + 1$
  * volume: $\pi[(x^2 + 2)^2 - (\frac{1}{2}x + 1)^2]dx$
* **Step 3:** Evaluate the definite integral over the interval $[0, 1]$[cite: 2]:
  $$V = \int_{0}^{1} \pi \left[ (x^2 + 2)^2 - \left(\frac{1}{2}x + 1\right)^2 \right] dx$$
  $$= \pi \int_{0}^{1} \left( x^4 + \frac{15}{4}x^2 - x + 3 \right) dx$$
  $$= \pi \left[ \frac{x^5}{5} + \frac{15}{4}\left(\frac{x^3}{3}\right) - \frac{x^2}{2} + 3x \right]_0^1 = \frac{79\pi}{20} \approx 12.4$$[cite: 2]

### Example 4
Find the volume of the solid generated by revolving the region described in Example 3 about the line $y = 3$[cite: 2].

**Solution**
* **Step 1:** Identify the outer and inner radii relative to the axis $y = 3$[cite: 2]:
  * thickness of washer: $dx$
  * outer radius: $3 - (\frac{1}{2}x + 1) = 2 - \frac{1}{2}x$
  * inner radius: $3 - (x^2 + 2) = 1 - x^2$
  * volume: $\pi \left[ \left(2 - \frac{1}{2}x\right)^2 - (1 - x^2)^2 \right] dx$
* **Step 2:** Set up and evaluate the integral[cite: 2]:
  $$V = \int_{0}^{1} \pi \left[ \left(2 - \frac{1}{2}x\right)^2 - (1 - x^2)^2 \right] dx$$
  $$= \pi \int_{0}^{1} \left( 3 - 2x + \frac{9}{4}x^2 - x^4 \right) dx$$
  $$= \pi \left[ 3x - x^2 + \frac{9}{4}\left(\frac{x^3}{3}\right) - \frac{x^5}{5} \right]_0^1$$
  $$= \frac{51\pi}{20} \approx 8.01$$[cite: 2]

### Example 5
The region in the first quadrant bounded by the graphs of $y = \frac{1}{8}x^3$ and $y = 2x$ is revolved about the y-axis[cite: 2]. Find the volume of the resulting solid[cite: 2].

**Solution**
* **Step 1:** Solve equations for $x$ in terms of $y$ to integrate with respect to $y$: $x = 2y^{1/3}$ and $x = \frac{1}{2}y$[cite: 2].
* **Step 2:** Use horizontal washers[cite: 2]:
  * thickness of washer: $dy$
  * outer radius: $2y^{1/3}$
  * inner radius: $\frac{1}{2}y$
  * volume: $\pi[(2y^{1/3})^2 - (\frac{1}{2}y)^2]dy = \pi(4y^{2/3} - \frac{1}{4}y^2)dy$
* **Step 3:** Evaluate the integral from $y = 0$ to $y = 8$[cite: 2]:
  $$V = \int_{0}^{8} \pi \left( 4y^{2/3} - \frac{1}{4}y^2 \right) dy$$
  $$= \pi \left[ \frac{12}{5}y^{5/3} - \frac{1}{12}y^3 \right]_0^8 = \frac{512}{15}\pi \approx 107.2$$[cite: 2]
